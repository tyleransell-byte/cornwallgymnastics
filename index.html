Policies

Privacy policy

At CGC we take your privacy seriously and will make sure your personal information is kept secure. We provide gymnastics activities and are registered with CAGA, SWAGA and British Gymnastics, who govern the sport and offer competitions and events in which we may take part.

Why we use your information

We need to process information about you or your child for these reasons:

  • Risk assessments. Some people have a medical condition that may be a risk when taking part in gymnastics or trampolining, so it's vital to share any relevant medical information. Where this applies, we'll ask for your agreement before carrying out any appropriate assessments.
  • To provide the gymnastics activity you've asked for, to talk to you about it and about changes to our terms and conditions, and to process payments where relevant.
  • To meet the aims of the club and make sure your child is well supported and safe while taking part in gymnastics.
  • To respond to any questions or complaints you send us.
  • To keep class attendance records and contact details for emergencies.
  • To film and take photos for coaching, and to promote the club on our website, social media and in our communications. Film and photos of children are only ever published in line with our safeguarding policy.
  • Legal reasons. To comply with the law and protect the club's interests and legal rights, including legal claims, compliance and regulatory activity.
  • Marketing. With your consent, we may email you or use social media to tell you about club news, activities, products and opportunities we think you'll be interested in. You can ask us to stop at any time.

You have the right to object to any of these uses of your information by contacting us. In some cases this may affect our ability to do the things needed for you or your child to take part in gymnastics.

Why we share your information

We won't share your information with any other organisation except with your consent, or in exceptional or emergency circumstances where we believe sharing it is vital to protect you, your child or another person.

If you or your child want to enter a competition run by an external organisation, we'll share the information needed to enter, usually name, date of birth and gender, with your agreement. Most competitions are large events, and there are likely to be photographers and people filming.

Your rights

You have the right to see the personal information we keep about you. This is called a subject access request. To make one, contact the Centre's Data Protection Officer at hello@cornwallgymnastics.uk. You'll need to provide two forms of identification, such as a driving licence, utility bill or passport.

If we hold other information about you, you can ask us to delete it or correct anything inaccurate. We'll either make the changes or explain why we aren't.

How long we keep information

If you leave the club, you can update your British Gymnastics registration by logging in to 'My Account'. The information you provided will then be archived for 60 days, during which you can transfer it to another club. After that it will be deleted, unless there's a legitimate and lawful reason to keep it.

Without a legitimate and lawful reason, information is deleted after 12 months unless you re-join the club within that time.

Changes to this notice

We keep our privacy notice under regular review. It was last updated on 18 May 2022.

Safeguarding & welfare

Cornwall Gymnastics Centre is fully committed to safeguarding and promoting the wellbeing of all its members, and to making sure the gym club is a place where everyone can enjoy themselves and reach their full potential.

Club Welfare Officer

Our Club Welfare Officer is Zoe Keeble. If you have any concern about a child's safety or wellbeing, please contact her by emailing welfare@cornwallgymnasticscentre.com.

Dropping off and collecting

  • Children are only in CGC's care once they've registered for their class and been allowed into the gym.
  • Always bring your child into the building and collect them from inside at the end of their session. Children should not be left in, or collected from, the car park alone.
  • Tell reception if someone else is collecting your child, and leave an emergency contact.
  • Gymnasts stay with their coaches at the end of a session until a parent or guardian collects them, and must never leave a session without telling the coach.
  • If you're delayed, please call us on 01208 814698.

Photography and filming

Taking photos or videos inside the gym is not allowed at any time, for child safeguarding reasons.

Raising a concern

We want every member to feel safe, happy and well. Gymnasts and parents are encouraged to be open and share any concerns or complaints about any part of the club. Complaints and important concerns should be raised with the Manager in writing by email, so they can be logged and dealt with professionally and promptly.

Cornwall Gymnastics Centre has the right to ask anyone not following our code of conduct to leave a session or, for ongoing problems, to leave the club.

Code of conduct

We have a code of conduct so that all gymnasts, coaches, administrators and parents know what's expected of them, to keep everyone safe and well.

Gymnasts

As a member of CGC, all gymnasts are expected to:

  • Take part within the rules and respect coaches, judges and their decisions.
  • Train with a positive attitude and always be kind to others.
  • Listen to their coach and concentrate in lessons.
  • Respect, support and encourage opponents and fellow club members. Bullying of any kind will not be tolerated.
  • Arrive 10 minutes before the session starts, ready for a prompt start, and register at reception before entering the gym.
  • Keep to agreed times for training and competitions, and tell their coach in advance if they expect to be late.
  • Not smoke, drink alcohol, take drugs or chew gum on club premises or when representing the club at competitions or other events.
  • Treat all equipment with respect, and take rubbish home or put it in the bins provided.
  • Tell their coach about any injuries or illness before the warm-up begins.
  • Stay with their parent or guardian until class begins, and stay with coaches at the end of a session until collected.
  • Be open and share any concerns or complaints about any part of the club.

What to wear

  • Hair neat, with long fringes clipped back out of the eyes and long hair tied up in a bun.
  • All jewellery removed before entering the gym. New ear piercings should be taped for the 6-week healing period, after which earrings must be removed before sessions.
  • A CGC t-shirt or leotard with fitted leggings or shorts in black, grey, white or CGC colours. In cold weather, a warm jumper to warm up in.
  • No baggy joggers, jeans, crop tops, or t-shirts with zips, buttons or pockets, as these aren't safe to train in.

Parents

As a parent of a CGC member, you're expected to:

  • Encourage your child to learn the rules and take part within them.
  • Make sure your child arrives 5 minutes before their class, with a bottle of water. No other drinks are allowed in the gym.
  • Give us the correct contact details and tell us about any changes.
  • Tell us if your child has an illness or injury that may stop them attending or performing as usual. Absences should be authorised by email or phone.
  • Make sure your child has any medication with them at each session, such as an inhaler or EpiPen, and tell us about any medical needs well before they attend.
  • Set a good example with appropriate language, good sportsmanship and applause for everyone's performances.
  • Help your child recognise good performance by encouraging effort as well as progress and results.
  • Keep good communication with coaches and officials. Coaches are happy to discuss feedback or questions at an agreed time outside training sessions.
  • Avoid talking to your child during the session where possible, unless it's an emergency.
  • Make sure your child waits seated in the waiting room until the coach calls them in.
  • When watching from the waiting and viewing area, keep any other children with you supervised, quiet and seated. If a class is disrupted, you'll politely be asked to leave.
  • Stay in the building during sessions if your child needs help going to the toilet.
  • Not enter the gym at any time unless a coach asks you to.
  • Make sure children arrive in clean clothes, with clean feet and hands, so no equipment is marked or damaged.
  • Follow the parking guidance in the Welcome Pack, to keep all children safe entering and leaving.

Parents who don't follow the code of conduct will be reminded of the behaviour CGC expects. In some circumstances, or after repeated breaches, members may be asked to leave the club, and fees paid will not be refunded.

Anti-bullying

CGC aims to provide a caring, friendly and safe environment for members, families, coaches and volunteers. Everyone should feel relaxed, secure and able to speak up.

Bullying is not tolerated

We do not tolerate or condone bullying in any form. This includes:

  • Physical bullying: pushing, pinching, kicking, spitting or similar behaviour.
  • Verbal bullying: name-calling, sarcasm, teasing, ridicule or deliberately ignoring someone.
  • Discriminatory bullying: abuse or harassment linked to sexuality, religion, gender, ability or other prejudice.
  • Cyberbullying: online bullying of gymnasts, coaches, parents or volunteers, whether it happens at the club or elsewhere.

Tell us about a concern

Please speak to a coach, the club secretary or a Welfare Officer straight away. You can report something even if you are not the person being bullied.

If you would rather not speak openly, email hello@cornwallgymnastics.uk, call a member of club staff or use the club’s complaints procedure.

What happens next

All concerns are formally logged and passed to our Welfare Officers, who decide how they should be handled. We tell the person raising the concern what action will be taken and how it will be followed up.

Where Welfare Officers consider a case serious, parents will be informed and invited to a meeting. Bullying behaviour and threats of bullying will be investigated and dealt with as promptly as possible.

Equality & inclusion

CGC supports equality of opportunity. Everyone involved in gymnastics, or wishing to become involved, should be able to do so in an environment free from discrimination. We value the different backgrounds and experiences people bring to our club.

Fair treatment for everyone

Everyone must respect each person’s rights, dignity, worth and right to make their own decisions. Staff, members, volunteers, participants and job applicants are entitled to fair treatment.

Our policy covers age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race (including colour, nationality and ethnic or national origins), religion or belief, sex and sexual orientation. It also recognises parental or marital status and social or economic background.

Removing barriers

Equal opportunity means making services accessible and adapting to individual needs. Sometimes additional effort, resources or positive action are needed to address barriers, past inequalities or under-representation.

We are committed to reasonable adjustments for disabled people, including removing, adapting or altering physical features where needed. We aim to anticipate barriers rather than wait for someone to encounter them. Rules may be adapted for people with disabilities or additional needs where this does not harm the standard, quality or integrity of the regulations.

Our responsibilities

  • Put this policy into practice and encourage involvement from all communities in participation, coaching, officiating and management.
  • Use good practice in recruitment, training and supervision of employees and volunteers.
  • Include equality in our strategy, development plans, services and organisational culture.
  • Review practices, procedures and relevant data; assess the equality impact of policies where appropriate.
  • Build an evidence base to guide priorities and measure progress, and increase awareness and commitment throughout the club.
  • Respond to concerns using the appropriate complaints, disciplinary and appeals procedures.

Behaviour we do not accept

  • Direct discrimination: treating someone less favourably because of a protected characteristic.
  • Indirect discrimination: a rule or practice applied to everyone that disadvantages people with a particular protected characteristic.
  • Discrimination by association or perception: unfair treatment because of someone’s connection to another person, or because of a characteristic others believe they have.
  • Discrimination arising from disability: unfavourable treatment connected to a person’s disability.
  • Bullying: intimidating, malicious, insulting or offensive behaviour, including misuse of power to undermine, humiliate or injure someone.
  • Harassment: unwanted or offensive conduct.
  • Victimisation: detrimental treatment for making, intending to make or supporting a complaint.

Speak up without fear

Raise concerns through CGC’s Complaints and Disciplinary Procedure. Nobody should be treated unfairly for raising a concern in good faith or supporting someone else who does.

Everyone associated with CGC is responsible for supporting this commitment. Individuals may be personally liable for unlawful discrimination, and serious harassment or victimisation may constitute a criminal offence. Breaches are treated seriously and may result in formal action under the British Gymnastics and CGC complaints and disciplinary procedures.

Keeping the policy effective

CGC’s policy commits to complying with the Equality Act 2010 and relevant subsequent legislation, seeking advice when reviewing the policy. It is made available to staff, members and volunteers through the website and other communications.

We monitor the policy regularly and undertake a full review every three years, or sooner following legislative changes, changes in the sport’s governance or a significant case.

Health & safety

The health, safety and welfare of our members are a priority. CGC works, so far as is reasonably practicable, to maintain a safe and healthy environment for members and others affected by our activities.

Gymnastics involves inherent risks. We take reasonable steps to minimise those risks, meet our duty of care and put appropriate health and safety arrangements in place. Staff are expected to help us uphold these standards.

Arriving and leaving safely

  • Use the designated parking bays in our car park.
  • Do not park on the road beside Preci Spark or near its barriers.
  • If the car park is full, park on the road by Sticky Fingers/Camel Glass and walk carefully to the gym.
  • Wear shoes when crossing the car park.
  • Escort children to and from the premises; do not drop them outside. The supplied club policy states that CGC is not responsible for accidents before entering the facility.

Everyone’s responsibilities

  • Take reasonable care of your own health, safety and welfare and that of anyone affected by your actions.
  • Carry out activities in line with your training and British Gymnastics policy and guidance.
  • Co-operate with CGC, British Gymnastics and the relevant home country or regional organisations on health, safety and welfare.
  • Follow reporting procedures for accidents and incidents, and inform British Gymnastics of serious allegations or concerns about poor practice.
  • Sign and return this policy when requested to confirm that you have read it, understood it and agree to follow the club’s expectations.

Report a concern immediately

Tell the Head Coach or a Welfare Officer straight away about health and safety concerns, including anyone’s access to or from the premises.

Photography & filming

This is a plain-language guide to the British Gymnastics Photography Policy supplied to CGC (version 1, March 2020), covering photos and video at training, competitions, events and other gymnastics activities.

Recording gymnastics should safeguard participants, respect privacy and present the sport positively. The policy aims to make photography safe, rather than prohibit it altogether. Venue and event rules must also be followed.

Protecting gymnasts

  • Never take, use or edit images in a way that is inappropriate for someone’s age or the setting, puts a child at risk, causes distress or embarrassment, or damages the sport’s reputation.
  • No photography or filming in toilets, showers or changing facilities, including by participants.
  • Check imagery before publishing it and respect any legal or safeguarding restriction, including court orders preventing publication.
  • Do not publish children’s personal information beyond their name and club, unless the policy’s public-interest exception applies. Using a first name rather than a full name is recommended.
  • Use safeguards to limit unauthorised viewing or use, and review website and social media images and captions regularly.

Club photography and privacy

Before taking images on the club’s behalf, explain the plans to the people featured and, where appropriate, their parents or guardians. CGC’s registration form records photography consent; tell us about any restrictions or concerns.

Photography must have an appropriate lawful basis, with clear information about its use. Images must be stored securely and kept only as long as necessary. Club imagery must not be stored on portable equipment without encryption; personal devices and personal social media accounts are for purely personal photography, not club imagery.

Images used for marketing involving or aimed at children must meet applicable advertising standards. Use this policy alongside safeguarding, health and safety and data protection guidance.

Personal photos and sharing

The supplied policy excludes appropriate images kept purely for personal purposes and not published or shared, except within family and close friends. Posting on websites or social media generally counts as publication, even when access is restricted. Safeguarding and venue rules still apply.

Live streaming

Live streaming is not permitted, except at major events with prior written British Gymnastics approval. Images sent directly to a monitor inside a gym or event to provide an open training environment are treated separately. Participants and visitors should be informed of approved filming or broadcasts.

At club, local and regional events

  • Organisers should communicate photography rules clearly, including notices, registration information or event guidance.
  • Official photographers should be identifiable and follow the policy. Designated photography areas may be provided.
  • Commercial photography providers need a formal agreement and appropriate checks, covering safeguarding, image rights, conduct, services and commercial arrangements. Approved providers should be identified in event information.
  • Report concerns to the organiser or Welfare Officer. If inappropriate photography is suspected, organisers may ask someone to leave, surrender film or delete images, and seek help from security or police.

At British Gymnastics events

Photography for purposes other than personal use requires British Gymnastics permission and advance media accreditation, including photographers attending on a club’s behalf. Accreditation, access and equipment permissions are determined by British Gymnastics; applications need not be accepted on the day.

Photography is generally restricted to public areas, such as spectator seating and concourses. Private or non-public areas require the relevant specific permissions. Competition areas are not open for photography by spectators; designated photo areas may be provided.

For non-accredited photographers:

  • Use personal or consumer equipment, rather than professional or commercial equipment. Organisers decide disputed equipment questions.
  • No telephoto zoom lenses; lenses over 135mm are generally prohibited, subject to the organiser’s rules.
  • No tripods, monopods or equipment stands, flash or additional lighting.
  • Do not connect photography equipment to external power supplies.
  • Keep equipment and bags clear of aisles, stairs, seating and emergency routes.

British Gymnastics may review images, prohibit unsuitable equipment or ask someone to stop taking photos, delete images or leave. Venue rules and relevant international federation rules may override event arrangements.

Concerns and review

Speak to the club’s Welfare Officer or event organiser if you are worried about photography. At British Gymnastics events, concerns can also be raised with the Event Welfare Officer, venue security or the British Gymnastics Integrity Unit. Significant breaches are handled through British Gymnastics complaints and disciplinary procedures.

The source policy provides for review when legislation, technology or governance requirements change. Further data protection and photography guidance is available through British Gymnastics’ GymNet resources.
